“One gallon per room” fails as soon as the ceiling is tall or the color goes from white to deep green. A **paint calculator** starts from the surfaces you will actually cover.

Perimeter times wall height is the gross wall area: 2 × (length + width) × height. Subtract openings. A typical door is about 21 square feet and a typical window about 15. Add the ceiling (length × width) only if you are painting it. Multiply by the number of coats, then divide by coverage. Many interior paints list about 350 square feet per gallon on a smooth, sealed wall. Fresh drywall and a drastic color change need the second coat in the math, not as a surprise.

The [Paint Calculator](/calculators/paint-calculator) rounds the purchase up to the next quarter gallon so you are not left with a formula that says 1.1 gallons and a store that sells by the quart. It also prices the cans from the per-gallon figure you enter.

Measure the floor first if you are unsure of length and width — the [Square Footage Calculator](/calculators/square-footage-calculator) handles rectangles and odd shapes. Buy the same batch number for one room. A calculator cannot fix a color shift between two cans mixed on different days.
