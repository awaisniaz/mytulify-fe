**Fixed deposit vs SIP** is one of the most common money questions for first-time savers. Both can build a larger corpus over time, but they answer different needs: an FD prioritizes predictable bank interest; a SIP prioritizes disciplined investing into market-linked funds. The right choice depends on your goal timeline, risk tolerance, and how soon you might need the cash.

This guide stays practical — no fake “average return” claims — and shows how to compare options with free calculators.

## Quick contrast

| Factor | Fixed deposit (FD) | Mutual fund SIP |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Cash flow | Usually one lump sum up front | Fixed amount every month |
| Return type | Contracted interest rate (subject to bank terms) | Market-linked; not guaranteed |
| Risk | Generally lower credit/interest-product risk | Market risk (NAV can fall) |
| Liquidity | Lock-in; early exit often penalized | Usually redeemable (exit loads may apply) |
| Best mental model | “Park money for a known payout” | “Buy units regularly and stay invested” |

Use an [FD calculator](/calculators/fd-calculator) when you already have a lump sum and a rate sheet. Use a [SIP calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator) when you want to model monthly contributions at an *assumed* expected return.

## When an FD usually fits better

Choose a fixed deposit when:

- You need a **known maturity amount** for a near-term goal (fees, down payment buffer, emergency reserve you will not touch).  
- You cannot tolerate seeing the balance fall in a bad market month.  
- You already have idle cash and want it to earn interest instead of sitting in a zero-interest account.

Run the bank’s rate through the [FD Calculator](/calculators/fd-calculator): principal, rate, tenure, and compounding (often quarterly) → maturity and interest.

### Example FD scenario

₹2,00,000 for 2 years at 7% with quarterly compounding. Plug those figures into the FD calculator and note maturity vs interest. That number is what you are “buying” with the lock-in — compare it to keeping the money liquid.

## When a SIP usually fits better

Choose a SIP when:

- Your goal is **years away** and you can keep investing through ups and downs.  
- You want to invest from monthly income rather than one large deposit.  
- You accept that actual returns will differ from any assumed rate in a calculator.

The [SIP Calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator) estimates maturity under a constant assumed annual return (and optional yearly step-up). That is a planning aid, not a forecast of a specific fund.

### Example SIP scenario

₹10,000 per month for 5 years at an assumed 12% annual return. Use the SIP calculator to see total invested, estimated gains, and maturity. Then stress-test with a lower assumed rate (for example 8%) so you are not planning only for an optimistic case.

## How to compare them fairly

1. **Match the time horizon.** A 1-year FD and a 10-year SIP are not rivals.  
2. **Separate guaranteed vs assumed.** FD interest is contractual (within product terms). SIP calculator rates are assumptions you choose.  
3. **Account for inflation.** A safe FD that barely beats inflation may preserve purchasing power poorly over long horizons — check scenarios with the [Inflation Calculator](/calculators/inflation-calculator).  
4. **Look backward separately from forward.** If you already held a fund, summarize past growth with [CAGR](/calculators/cagr-calculator). Do not confuse historical CAGR with a future SIP assumption.

## A simple decision checklist

- Need the money in under ~2–3 years and hate volatility → lean **FD** (or a mix with a liquid buffer).  
- Building wealth for a distant goal and can invest monthly → lean **SIP**, sized so you can continue in bad months.  
- Have both a lump sum *and* monthly surplus → many people use an FD for near-term needs and a SIP for long-term growth — run both calculators instead of forcing one product to do every job.

## Bottom line

**FD vs SIP** is not a popularity contest. An FD is for predictable, locked savings at a stated rate. A SIP is for systematic market investing with uncertain outcomes.
