Choosing between an **RD**, an **FD**, and a **SIP** is less about which product is “best” and more about how you save, how much risk you accept, and when you need the money. Use each product’s calculator to stress-test numbers — not to predict markets or guarantee bank payouts.

## Side-by-side snapshot

| | Recurring deposit (RD) | Fixed deposit (FD) | Mutual fund SIP |
|--|------------------------|--------------------|-----------------|
| How you deposit | Fixed amount every month | Usually one lump sum | Fixed amount every month |
| Typical home | Bank / deposit product | Bank / deposit product | Mutual fund scheme |
| Return style | Stated deposit rate (product rules) | Stated deposit rate (product rules) | Market-linked; assumed rate in a calculator |
| Main risk | Credit / product terms; rate usually known | Same as RD for bank FDs | Market volatility |
| Best for | Building a corpus with monthly discipline at a known rate | Parking a lump sum for a known tenure | Long-horizon goals where you accept market risk |

## When an RD fits

Choose an RD when you want **monthly discipline** and a **known deposit rate**, and you are comfortable locking installments for the tenure. An [RD calculator](/calculators/rd-calculator) shows maturity, interest, and total deposited from installment, rate, and years/months.

RDs are a poor fit if you already have a large lump sum ready to invest (an FD may be simpler) or if you need equity-like growth over decades (a SIP models that differently).

## When an FD fits

Choose an FD when you have money **available today** and want a fixed-tenure deposit. The [FD calculator](/calculators/fd-calculator) estimates maturity from principal, rate, tenure, and compounding frequency (quarterly is common for many bank FDs).

If your cash arrives only each payday, an RD or SIP matches the cash-flow pattern better than forcing a one-time FD.

## When a SIP fits

A SIP invests a fixed sum into a mutual fund on a schedule. Returns are **not** guaranteed; a [SIP calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator) only projects outcomes at an **assumed** annual return so you can compare tenures and step-up scenarios.

Use a SIP mindset for long goals where you can stay invested through ups and downs. Use RD/FD mindset when capital protection and a stated deposit rate matter more than market upside.

## A simple decision path

1. **Lump sum ready now, want a deposit rate** → model an [FD](/calculators/fd-calculator).  
2. **Monthly surplus, want a bank deposit habit** → model an [RD](/calculators/rd-calculator).  
3. **Monthly surplus, long horizon, accept market risk** → model a [SIP](/calculators/sip-calculator).  
4. **Long-horizon tax-advantaged deposit-style savings (where available)** → also compare a [PPF calculator](/calculators/ppf-calculator) for yearly contribution math.

## Compare with real numbers (not vibes)

Run the same monthly amount through the RD and SIP tools with honest assumptions (bank RD rate vs a conservative expected SIP return). Run the same lump sum through the FD tool. Look at **total deposited vs projected maturity**, tenure, and whether you can stick to the plan if income dips.

None of these calculators is financial advice. Confirm bank rates and scheme documents before you invest or open an account.
