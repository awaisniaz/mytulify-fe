An **NPS calculator** helps you estimate how large your **National Pension System** corpus could be by retirement. Instead of guessing from a PRAN balance alone, you model monthly contributions, an assumed annual return, years until you stop contributing, optional contribution increases, and how much of the final pot you illustrate as a lump sum versus annuity purchase.

This is a **planning** view. Fund performance, asset allocation, fees, exit rules, and tax treatment are set by scheme authorities and market outcomes. Always confirm live balances on your NPS / CRA portal and treat calculator output as a scenario — not a guarantee.

## What an NPS calculator needs

| Input | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| Monthly contribution | Your Tier-I deposit (plus employer share if applicable) |
| Expected annual return (%) | Blended assumption for equity/debt mix you want to stress-test |
| Current age & retirement age | Defines contribution years |
| Annual step-up (%) | Models raising deposits as income grows |
| Opening balance | Existing NPS corpus before the projection window |
| Lump-sum % at retirement | Illustrates cash withdrawal vs annuity purchase split |

Open the [NPS Calculator](/calculators/nps-calculator) and fill these fields to see corpus, returns, total invested, and the lump-sum / annuity illustration.

## How the estimate is built (plain language)

1. Each month’s contribution is compounded forward at the monthly equivalent of your expected annual return.
2. If you set an annual step-up, the monthly deposit rises once per year from year 2 onward.
3. An opening balance (if any) grows for the full tenure and is added to contribution growth.
4. At retirement, the tool splits the projected corpus using your chosen lump-sum percentage; the remainder is shown as annuity purchase.

That is enough to compare scenarios — for example “flat ₹10,000/month to age 60” vs “5% step-up for 30 years” — without uploading account data.

### Worked planning example

Suppose:

- Monthly contribution: ₹10,000
- Expected return: enter whatever blended rate you are modeling
- Ages: 30 → 60 (30 years)
- Step-up: 5% per year
- Opening balance: ₹0
- Lump-sum illustration: 60%

Month 1 deposits are fixed. Over three decades, step-ups raise later contributions, so total invested and estimated returns both climb. Run the same numbers in the [NPS Calculator](/calculators/nps-calculator) and change one variable at a time (return, step-up, or retirement age) to see which lever moves corpus most for *your* plan.

## Lump-sum vs annuity — what the split means

NPS exit rules often require a portion of the corpus to be used to **purchase an annuity** that pays a pension, while a portion may be taken as a **lump sum**. Online planners commonly illustrate a 60% / 40% style split, but requirements can depend on corpus size and current regulations. Use the percentage field to match the rule set you are planning against — the calculator does not invent your legal entitlement.
