A **PPF calculator** answers a planning question: if you contribute a fixed amount each year to a Public Provident Fund account at an assumed annual interest rate, what could the maturity value look like? That projection helps you set a contribution habit, compare tenures (including extensions), and see how much of the corpus is interest versus deposits — before you lock in money.

## What Public Provident Fund (PPF) is

PPF is a long-horizon savings scheme commonly used for retirement and goal-based planning. You typically contribute yearly (within scheme limits), earn interest that compounds annually under the scheme’s rules, and hold the account for a standard block of years that can often be extended.

An online **PPF maturity calculator** does **not** replace your bank or post office statement. It applies a clear yearly compounding model so you can stress-test “what if” scenarios — for example different contribution amounts or a longer tenure after extension.

## How this PPF calculator models growth

Mytulify’s tool uses a common planning model:

1. Start with an optional **opening balance** (use `0` for a new account).  
2. Each year, add your **yearly contribution** at the start of the year.  
3. Apply **annual interest**: `balance = (balance + contribution) × (1 + rate)`.  
4. Repeat for the number of **tenure years**.

Then:

- **Total invested** = opening balance + sum of yearly contributions  
- **Interest earned** = maturity − total invested  
- **Maturity value** = final balance  

Actual PPF interest is notified periodically and credited under detailed account rules (including how mid-year deposits are treated). Always enter the rate you want to model and confirm final figures with your provider.

## Why contribution, rate, and tenure matter

| Input | What it changes |
|-------|-----------------|
| Yearly contribution | Scales how much you put in each year and how large the final corpus can become |
| Annual interest rate % | Compounding sensitivity — small rate differences grow over long tenures |
| Tenure (years) | Time in the account; 15 years is the classic block, extensions add more years |
| Opening balance | Lets existing account holders project from today’s balance plus future deposits |

Use a conservative rate for goals you cannot miss. Run a second scenario at a slightly higher rate only as an upside case — not as a guarantee.
