A **lumpsum calculator** answers a practical investing question: if you invest a fixed amount once at an assumed annual return for a set tenure, what maturity value and gains should you expect? Fund houses and planners publish expected-return scenarios; the calculator turns those numbers into a clear corpus estimate so you can compare tenures before you commit.

## What a lumpsum investment is

A lumpsum is a **one-time** mutual fund investment — you put in a single principal and leave it invested for the tenure. Unlike a [SIP](/calculators/sip-calculator), you do not add money every month. Interest (or market returns) compounds on the full amount from day one.

An online calculator does **not** predict a specific fund’s future NAV. It applies standard compound-growth math so you can sanity-check goals, tenures, and assumed rates.

## The common lumpsum maturity formulas

### Yearly compounding (typical for MF planners)

Many lumpsum planners use:

**A = P × (1 + r)^t**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| P | One-time investment amount |
| r | Expected annual return as a decimal (e.g. 12% → 0.12) |
| t | Tenure in years (fractional years allowed) |
| A | Maturity (future) value |

**Estimated returns** = A − P

### Monthly compounding

Some planners compound more frequently at the same annual rate:

**A = P × (1 + r/12)^(12 × t)**

Monthly compounding produces a slightly higher maturity than yearly compounding at the same headline annual rate, because gains are reinvested twelve times per year.

## Worked example

- Investment: ₹1,00,000
- Expected return: 12% per year
- Tenure: 10 years
- Method: yearly compounding

A = 1,00,000 × (1.12)^10

Total invested stays ₹1,00,000. Maturity is higher because the full principal compounds for the entire decade. Plug the same inputs into the [Lumpsum Calculator](/calculators/lumpsum-calculator) to see invested amount, estimated returns, and maturity instantly in your browser.

## How to use the calculator well

1. Use a **conservative** expected return for planning and a second, higher scenario for sensitivity — do not treat one rate as a promise.
2. Match **tenure** to when you actually need the money (goal date), not an arbitrary round number.
3. If you will invest monthly instead of once, switch to the [SIP Calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator).
4. To reverse-engineer the rate that took a past start value to an end value, use the [CAGR Calculator](/calculators/cagr-calculator).
