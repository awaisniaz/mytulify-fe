**Grammarly** is an editor that lives in your browser and docs. **ChatGPT** is a drafter that rewrites from a prompt. People google “Grammarly vs ChatGPT” because they overlap on “make this email better” — but the jobs are different.

If the copy is confidential (client NDA, unpublished research), prefer **local** tools first. Mytulify text utilities run in your browser with no account.

## Snapshot comparison

| Factor | Grammarly | ChatGPT |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Core job | Correct + tone-check existing text | Generate or heavily rewrite |
| Where it sits | Extensions, Docs, desktop app | Chat app / API |
| Strength | Grammar, clarity, consistency as you type | Structure, brainstorms, full drafts |
| Weakness | Weak at inventing a whole article from a one-liner | Can “fix” voice into generic AI tone |

## Grammarly — keep your words, clean the edges

Use Grammarly when the draft **already exists**: emails, proposals, LinkedIn posts, student papers. It flags agreement, punctuation, and optional tone. Premium/Business add more suggestions and brand tones.

Choose Grammarly when:

- You write all day in Gmail or Google Docs
- You want inline red underlines, not a separate chat tab
- Legal/comms teams want a consistent “correctness” layer

Try it: <a href="https://www.grammarly.com/" rel="sponsored nofollow">Grammarly</a>

## ChatGPT — start from a blank page

Use ChatGPT when you need a **first draft**, an outline, or a rewrite in another tone (“make this friendlier, keep the facts”). It is the wrong default for “just fix commas” — it will often rewrite sentences you wanted to keep.

Choose ChatGPT when:

- You are stuck on structure
- You need variants (3 subject lines, 2 CTAs)
- You will **edit hard** before sending
