An **SCSS calculator** helps you estimate how a **Senior Citizen Savings Scheme** deposit can pay interest when you lock a lump sum at an assumed annual rate for a chosen tenure. Unlike many fixed deposits that compound interest into the maturity amount, SCSS planning usually focuses on **quarterly interest payouts** and the **principal returned at maturity**.

This is a **planning** view. Eligibility (age and related rules), deposit limits, premature closure, extension options, tax treatment, and the notified rate depend on current scheme rules. Always confirm with your bank or post office and a qualified advisor — calculator output is a scenario, not an official statement.

## What an SCSS calculator needs

| Input | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| Deposit amount (lump sum) | One-time principal you plan to place in SCSS |
| Annual interest rate (%) | The notified SCSS rate you want to model |
| Tenure — years | How long the deposit stays invested (often modeled as 5 years) |
| Extra months (optional) | Partial-year modeling when you need finer tenure control |

## How the interest estimate is built (plain language)

Mytulify’s tool uses a common **quarterly payout** planning model:

1. Start with your **lump-sum deposit**.
2. Compute **annual interest** as `deposit × (rate ÷ 100)`.
3. Split that into **quarterly interest**: `annual interest ÷ 4`.
4. Multiply annual interest by tenure years for **total interest** received over the account life.
5. Treat **principal at maturity** as the original deposit (interest was paid out, not compounded in).
6. Show **total payout** as principal + all interest received across the tenure.

That payout pattern is what makes SCSS projections feel different from a compounding FD maturity calculator.

### Worked planning example

Suppose:

- Deposit: ₹15,00,000
- Rate: 8.2% per year
- Tenure: 5 years

Annual interest is ₹1,23,000. Quarterly interest is ₹30,750. Over five years, total interest is ₹6,15,000. At maturity, the modeled principal returned is ₹15,00,000, and total payout (principal + interest) is ₹21,15,000. ## Why “principal at maturity” often equals the deposit

If interest compounds into the account, maturity grows above the original principal. In the standard SCSS payout model, interest leaves the account each quarter, so maturity planning centers on:

- Steady **income-style** quarterly credits
- **Principal returned** when the tenure ends
- **Total cash received** = principal + sum of all quarterly interest
