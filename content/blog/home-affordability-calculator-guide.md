Affordability runs backward from a mortgage payment. You pick a housing share of gross income — 28% is a common ceiling — and you also keep total debts, including that housing payment, inside a back-end cap such as 36%. The lower of those two monthly limits is the budget. A **home affordability calculator** turns that budget, a down payment, a rate, and a term into a price.

Taxes and insurance scale with the price, so the loan is not “budget × term.” Each guess of price creates a loan (price minus down payment), a principal-and-interest payment, and a tax-and-insurance slice. The [Home Affordability Calculator](/calculators/home-affordability-calculator) adjusts the price until that housing total matches the budget.

Then test the specific house. The [Mortgage Calculator](/calculators/mortgage-calculator) uses that home’s real tax, insurance, HOA, and PMI. Recheck [debt-to-income](/calculators/debt-to-income-calculator) with the full payment. Upfront lender fees change APR more than they change the payment — see the [APR Calculator](/calculators/apr-calculator). This range is not a pre-approval.
