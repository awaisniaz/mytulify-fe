A **gratuity calculator** helps you estimate the lump-sum amount that may be payable when you leave employment after qualifying service — typically at resignation, retirement, or other exits covered by your employer’s policy or the Payment of Gratuity Act. Instead of guessing from a final payslip, you model **last drawn salary**, **years (and months) of service**, and which formula applies.

This is a **planning** view. Eligibility, wage definitions, rounding, tax treatment, and caps depend on statute and employer policy. Always confirm with payroll / HR and a qualified advisor — calculator output is a scenario, not a guarantee or legal opinion.

## What a gratuity calculator needs

| Input | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| Last drawn salary (basic + DA) | The wage base used in the standard formulas |
| Completed years + extra months | Defines service tenure used in the payout |
| Formula (÷26 vs ÷30) | Act-style vs private / non-Act illustration |
| Half-year rounding | Whether leftover months &gt; 6 count as a full year |
| Optional tax exemption ceiling | Illustrates how much sits under vs above a limit you enter |

## How the estimate is built (plain language)

1. **Act-style formula (÷26):** `(last drawn salary × 15 × service years) ÷ 26`
2. **Private / non-Act illustration (÷30):** same 15-days-per-year idea with a 30-day month divisor
3. **Service years:** completed years plus extra months; with half-year rounding, leftover months **greater than 6** typically round up to the next full year
4. **Optional ceiling:** the tool can split the estimate into “under your ceiling” vs “above” for tax planning sketches — you enter the ceiling; the tool does not invent current law

That is enough to compare scenarios — for example “10 years flat under ÷26” vs “10 years 8 months with rounding” — without uploading payroll data.

### Worked planning example

Suppose:

- Last drawn salary (basic + DA): ₹50,000
- Service: 10 years, 0 months
- Formula: Payment of Gratuity Act (÷26)
- Rounding: on

Daily wage illustration = 50,000 ÷ 26. Fifteen days × 10 years gives the numerator side of the Act formula. Run the same numbers in the [Gratuity Calculator](/calculators/gratuity-calculator) and change one variable at a time (salary, months, or ÷30 mode) to see which lever moves the estimate most for *your* case.

## Eligibility — why “5 years” shows up

Many Act-covered situations require roughly **five years of continuous service** before gratuity is payable on resignation or retirement. Death or disablement can follow different rules. If your modeled service is under five years, treat the amount as a **formula illustration only** until you confirm eligibility.
