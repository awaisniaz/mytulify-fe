Credit card interest is monthly. Each statement adds APR ÷ 12 to whatever balance is left, then your payment removes interest first and principal second. A **credit card payoff calculator** repeats that until the balance is gone and counts the months and the interest.

If the payment is less than or equal to that month’s interest, the balance never reaches zero. The tool should say so instead of printing a fake date. Raising the payment by a fixed extra amount shortens the term and cuts interest by more than the extra cash feels like, because later months accrue interest on a smaller balance.

Use the [Credit Card Payoff Calculator](/calculators/credit-card-payoff-calculator) with the APR on the statement, not a promotional rate that expires next month. For several cards, compare snowball and avalanche order in the [Debt Payoff Calculator](/calculators/debt-payoff-calculator). If a mortgage is next, those minimums belong in the [debt-to-income](/calculators/debt-to-income-calculator) total. APR on an installment loan is a different idea — fees versus rate — and lives in the [APR Calculator](/calculators/apr-calculator).
