**SIP** and **SWP** sound similar, but they move money in opposite directions. Confusing them leads to the wrong calculator, the wrong mandate, and cash-flow surprises. This guide keeps the distinction practical.

## Side-by-side: SIP vs SWP

| | SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) | SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan) |
|---|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Direction | You **add** money every month | You **take out** money every month |
| Starting point | Often small; corpus builds over time | Needs an existing corpus (lump sum or matured SIP) |
| Typical goal | Wealth creation, long-term goals | Income, retirement drawdown, goal payouts |
| Risk framing | Market dips can buy more units | Market dips can shrink the remaining pot faster |

Neither product “guarantees” a return. Both are scheduling tools around mutual fund units.

## When SIP is the better fit

Use a SIP when you have regular income and a future goal (house down payment, education, retirement corpus) and you want to build wealth gradually.

- Affordable monthly amount beats waiting for a large lump sum  
- Rupee-cost averaging spreads purchase timing across markets  
- Step-up SIPs can grow contributions as salary rises  

Estimate outcomes with the [SIP Calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator) before you commit.

## When SWP is the better fit

Use an SWP when you already have a corpus and need predictable cash without redeeming everything at once.

- Monthly living expenses in retirement  
- Funding a child’s fees for a fixed number of years  
- Drawing down a matured SIP or inheritance while keeping the rest invested  

Model sustainability with the [SWP Calculator](/calculators/swp-calculator) — especially whether the corpus lasts the full tenure at your planned withdrawal.

## Can you use both in one life plan?

Yes — often in sequence:

1. **Accumulation:** SIP for 10–20+ years to build a corpus.  
2. **Transition:** Stop or reduce SIPs; optionally park a buffer in cash or debt.  
3. **Distribution:** Start an SWP from the equity/hybrid corpus (or a mix) for income.

Some investors run a small SIP in one fund while taking an SWP from another. That only works if cash flow and risk capacity allow it — the calculators help you see the numbers; they do not replace a full plan.

## Shared planning mistakes

- Assuming past fund returns will continue exactly (stress-test lower rates).  
- Setting SWP withdrawals so high that the corpus dies mid-goal.  
- Ignoring inflation — today’s ₹25,000 monthly need may rise; pair thinking with an [inflation calculator](/calculators/inflation-calculator).  
- Comparing SIP maturity and SWP final corpus as if they answer the same question — one grows deposits; the other depletes (or sustains) a starting pot.

## Quick decision checklist

- Need to **build** a pot from salary? → SIP  
- Need to **draw** income from a pot? → SWP  
- Need both over a lifetime? → SIP first, SWP later (or separate funds)
