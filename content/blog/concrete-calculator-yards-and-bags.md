Ordering concrete by guesswork is how a Saturday pour stops halfway across the slab. A **concrete calculator** converts length, width, and thickness into cubic yards for a ready-mix truck, or into bags when the job is small enough to mix yourself.

## Slab volume

Measure length and width in feet and thickness in inches. Volume in cubic feet is:

length × width × (thickness ÷ 12)

Divide by 27 to get cubic yards. A 10 by 10 foot pad at 4 inches is about 1.23 cubic yards before waste. Round columns use the cylinder formula: π × radius² × height, with the diameter converted from inches to feet.

## Waste, bags, and cost

Add 5–10% for spillage, an uneven base, and forms that are a little wide. The [Concrete Calculator](/calculators/concrete-calculator) applies that percent, then divides by bag yield. A common 80 lb bag is about 0.60 cubic feet; a 60 lb bag is about 0.45. Multiply bags by the price on the pallet for a material total. Ready-mix is sold by the yard, so use the yard figure for a truck and the bag figure only for piers, steps, and patches.

Check the footprint first with the [Square Footage Calculator](/calculators/square-footage-calculator). If the base is gravel, size that layer with the [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator) before you set forms. Neither number includes labor, rebar, or the short-load fee a plant may charge for a small order.
