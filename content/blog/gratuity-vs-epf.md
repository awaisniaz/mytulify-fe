**Gratuity** and **EPF** (Employee Provident Fund) often appear together when salaried employees plan resignation, retirement, or a job change — but they are not the same benefit. Gratuity is typically a **one-time exit payout** tied to last drawn wages and years of service. EPF is typically a **running account** funded by monthly employee and employer contributions that grows with interest over your career.

This article is educational planning content, not tax, legal, or payroll advice. Scheme rules, tax treatment, eligibility, and employer policy change — verify with official sources, HR, and a qualified advisor when decisions involve real money.

## Snapshot comparison

| Factor | Gratuity | EPF |
|--------|----------|-----|
| What it usually is | Lump-sum payment at qualifying exit | Accumulated provident-fund balance |
| Main drivers | Last drawn salary (often basic + DA) × service years | Monthly contributions × interest × tenure |
| Typical formula feel | 15 days’ wages per year (÷26 under Act-style models) | Ongoing deposits credited with notified-style interest |
| Planning tool | [Gratuity Calculator](/calculators/gratuity-calculator) | [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator) |
| When you feel it most | At exit / settlement | Continuously on payslips; large at withdrawal / retirement |

Use the table as a lens for *your* exit package — not as a ranking of which benefit is “better.”

## When gratuity is the natural starting point

If you are modeling a **final settlement** question — *If my last basic + DA and service years stay in this ballpark, what gratuity might be illustrated?* — start with the [Gratuity Calculator](/calculators/gratuity-calculator).

Practical steps:

1. Note last drawn basic + dearness allowance from your payslip or offer letter definition.
2. Count completed years and leftover months of continuous service.
3. Choose Act-style ÷26 or private ÷30 if that matches the policy you are modeling.
4. Check whether half-year rounding applies in your scenario.

Deep dive on inputs and worked examples: [Gratuity Calculator Explained](/blog/gratuity-calculator-guide).

## When EPF is the natural starting point

If you care about **how the PF balance grows while you are still employed** — contributions, employer EPF share, assumed interest, salary hikes — use the [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator). EPF answers a different question than gratuity: it projects a **corpus from recurring deposits**, not a 15-days-per-year exit formula.

## How they fit together at exit

Many people receive **both** (when eligible): a gratuity settlement **and** EPF withdrawal or transfer options. They solve different parts of the package:

- **Gratuity** ≈ service × final wage formula
- **EPF** ≈ years of monthly saving with interest
