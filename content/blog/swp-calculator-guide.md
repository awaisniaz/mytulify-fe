An **SWP calculator** answers a practical planning question: if you start with a mutual fund corpus and withdraw a fixed amount every month at an assumed return, how much will you take out, how much growth might the remaining balance earn, and what could be left at the end?

That projection helps retirees, parents funding education, and anyone turning a lump sum into a steady cash flow — before they lock in a scheme’s SWP mandate.

## What a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) is

An SWP is the reverse habit of a SIP. Instead of investing a fixed sum every month, you begin with a corpus already invested and instruct the fund house to redeem a fixed amount (or units worth that amount) on a schedule — often monthly.

The unsold units stay invested, so the remaining corpus can still grow or shrink with the market. A calculator does **not** predict real NAVs. It assumes a constant annual return so you can stress-test “what if” withdrawal rates.

## How the SWP model works (month by month)

A common planning model used by online SWP tools:

1. Start with corpus **C**.
2. Each month, grow the balance at monthly rate **r** (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100).
3. Subtract fixed withdrawal **W**.
4. Repeat for **n** months (years × 12).

Over the full tenure:

- **Total withdrawn** ≈ sum of successful monthly payouts  
- **Final corpus** = balance after the last month  
- **Estimated returns** = total withdrawn + final corpus − initial corpus  

If a month’s balance cannot cover **W**, the plan depletes early: the tool withdraws what remains and stops.

₹10,00,000 corpus, ₹10,000 monthly withdrawal, 10% expected annual return, 10 years:

## Inputs that matter most

| Input | What it changes |
|-------|-----------------|
| Initial corpus | Starting runway for withdrawals and compounding |
| Monthly withdrawal | Cash flow vs longevity of the pot |
| Expected return % | How fast the remaining balance can refill |
| Tenure (years) | How long you need the cash flow to last |

High withdrawals look fine for a few years and fail later. Prefer a conservative expected return for goals you cannot miss (living expenses). Run a second scenario at a lower return to see if the plan still holds.

## Practical tips before you start an SWP

- Match withdrawal timing to when you actually need cash (rent, fees, living costs).
- Leave a cash buffer outside the SWP for emergencies so you are not forced to raise the withdrawal mid-market dip.
- Revisit the plan yearly — income needs and markets change.
- Pair SWP planning with a [SIP calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator) if you are still in the accumulation phase before withdrawals begin.

An SWP is a cash-flow tool, not a guarantee. Use the free calculator to set a withdrawal that your corpus can realistically support under the return assumptions you choose.
