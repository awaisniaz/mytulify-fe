An **EPF calculator** helps you estimate how much your **Employee Provident Fund** balance could grow from monthly contributions tied to your salary. Instead of guessing from a payslip line item, you model wages, contribution percentages, an assumed interest rate, and years of service — then see projected maturity, interest, and how much came from you vs your employer’s EPF share.

This is a **planning** view. Official interest rates, wage ceilings, EPS vs EPF splits, and withdrawal rules are set by scheme authorities and your employer. Always confirm live balances on your EPFO / UAN portal.

## What an EPF calculator needs

| Input | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| Monthly EPF wages | Usually basic + dearness allowance (check your payroll) |
| Employee contribution % | Often 12% of EPF wages |
| Employer contribution to EPF | Commonly illustrated as 3.67% when part of the employer share goes to EPS — or model full 12% / custom |
| Assumed annual interest rate | Enter the rate you want to stress-test; do not treat a default as the official notified rate |
| Years of service | How long you expect contributions to continue |
| Optional salary hike | Raises future monthly deposits if your wages grow |
| Optional opening balance | Existing PF balance before the projection window |

Open the [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator) and fill these fields to get maturity, interest earned, and contribution totals.

## How the estimate is built (plain language)

1. Each month, the tool adds **employee** and **employer (EPF)** deposits based on current monthly wages and your chosen percentages.
2. Interest is **accrued monthly** on the running balance and **credited once per year** in the model (a common planning approximation of yearly credit).
3. If you set an annual hike, wages (and therefore deposits) rise from year 2 onward.
4. Opening balance, if any, compounds alongside new deposits.

That is enough to compare scenarios — for example “flat salary for 15 years” vs “5% hike for 20 years” — without uploading payroll data.

### Worked planning example

Suppose:

- Monthly EPF wages: ₹50,000
- Employee: 12% → ₹6,000 / month
- Employer to EPF: 3.67% → ₹1,835 / month
- Assumed rate: enter whatever rate you are modeling
- Tenure: 20 years, with a modest yearly hike

Month 1 deposits are fixed from the percentages above. Over two decades, hikes raise later contributions, so total invested and interest both climb. Run the same numbers in the [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator) and change one variable at a time (rate, hike, or years) to see which lever moves maturity most for *your* plan.

## Employer 3.67% vs full 12% — which mode to pick?

Many guides illustrate employer contribution as **12% of wages**, with a portion directed to **EPS** (pension) and the rest to **EPF**. Online planners often default the EPF portion to about **3.67%** for that split. If your employer does not use that split, or you want a simplified “12% + 12% into PF” scenario, choose the matching mode or enter a **custom** employer EPF percentage.

The calculator never invents your company’s payroll policy — it only applies the percentages you select.
