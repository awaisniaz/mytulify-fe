An **FD calculator** (fixed deposit calculator) answers a practical question: if you lock in a principal today at a stated annual rate for a set tenure, what maturity amount and interest should you expect? Banks publish rates; the calculator turns those numbers into a clear payout estimate so you can compare offers before you commit.

## What a fixed deposit is

A fixed deposit is a bank (or NBFC) product where you place a lump sum for a fixed period and earn interest according to the product’s rules. You typically cannot withdraw early without a penalty. Unlike a [SIP](/calculators/sip-calculator), you usually deposit once up front rather than every month.

An online **fixed deposit calculator** does not replace your bank’s official quote. It applies standard interest math so you can sanity-check rate sheets and tenure options.

## The FD maturity formulas

### Compound interest (most bank FDs)

**A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t)**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| P | Principal (deposit amount) |
| r | Annual interest rate as a decimal (e.g. 7% → 0.07) |
| n | Compounding periods per year (4 = quarterly, 12 = monthly, 1 = yearly) |
| t | Tenure in years (e.g. 2 years 6 months → 2.5) |
| A | Maturity amount |

**Interest earned = A − P**

### Simple interest

**A = P × (1 + r × t)**

Use simple interest only when the product truly does not compound. Many retail bank FDs compound (often **quarterly**).

### Worked example

- Principal: ₹1,00,000  
- Rate: 7% per year  
- Tenure: 3 years  
- Compounding: quarterly (n = 4)  

A = 100000 × (1 + 0.07/4)^(4×3) ≈ **₹123,143**  
Interest ≈ **₹23,143**

## Why compounding frequency matters

At the same headline rate, **more frequent compounding** usually produces a slightly higher maturity amount because interest is added to the principal more often. That is why two FDs both advertised at “7%” can pay different amounts if one compounds monthly and another yearly.

When you compare banks:

1. Match tenure (years and months).  
2. Note whether interest is compounded or paid out periodically.  
3. Use the same compounding assumption in the calculator for a fair comparison.  
4. Confirm TDS, senior-citizen rates, and premature-withdrawal rules with the bank.

## Practical tips before you book an FD

- Prefer the bank’s official maturity schedule when amounts are large.  
- Check whether interest is reinvested or paid out (payout FDs need a different cash-flow view).  
- Compare after-tax outcomes if TDS applies in your country.  
- Revisit rates when rates are changing — a short calculator check can save a long lock-in at a weak rate.
