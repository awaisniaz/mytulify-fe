The number on a listing is not the number that leaves your account each month. A **mortgage calculator** that only shows principal and interest will make a house look cheaper than it is. The payment that matters is PITI: principal, interest, taxes, and insurance, plus HOA dues and private mortgage insurance when they apply.

## What goes into a real monthly housing payment

**Principal and interest** come from the loan amount, the note rate, and the term:

M = P × [r(1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n − 1]

P is the price minus your down payment. r is the monthly rate. n is the number of payments (360 for 30 years, 180 for 15).

**Property tax and homeowners insurance** are usually quoted per year. Divide by 12 and add them. **HOA fees** are already monthly. **PMI** often appears when the down payment is under 20% of the price. A planning rate near 0.5% of the loan per year is a starting point, not a quote. At 20% down, PMI in this estimate drops to zero.

## 15-year versus 30-year on the same loan

A shorter term raises the monthly principal-and-interest payment and cuts total interest. A longer term does the opposite. Run both on the same home price, rate, and down payment before you decide the lower payment is “cheaper.” The [Mortgage Calculator](/calculators/mortgage-calculator) puts those two columns next to each other and builds a year-by-year principal, interest, and balance table, including extra principal if you plan to pay more than the minimum.

## How to use the numbers

1. Enter the price and down payment as a percent or a dollar amount.
2. Use a rate you have actually seen, then try one step higher.
3. Add yearly tax and insurance, monthly HOA, and a PMI rate if you are under 20% down.
4. Check the full monthly figure, not only P&I.
5. If the payment is tight, test a lower price in the [Home Affordability Calculator](/calculators/home-affordability-calculator) and check [debt-to-income](/calculators/debt-to-income-calculator) before you talk to a lender.

Fees charged up front change the true cost of borrowing. Compare that with the [APR Calculator](/calculators/apr-calculator). The result is a budget test, not an approval.
