**EPF** (Employee Provident Fund) and **PPF** (Public Provident Fund) both help people think in terms of long-horizon provident-style savings — but they are not the same product. EPF is typically tied to **employment and payroll**. PPF is typically a **voluntary account** you fund on your own schedule (within scheme rules). Choosing between them — or using both — starts with understanding cash flow and who can contribute, then estimating maturity with the right calculator.

This article is educational planning content, not tax, legal, or investment advice. Scheme limits, interest notifications, tax treatment, and eligibility change — verify with official sources and a qualified advisor when decisions involve real money.

## Snapshot comparison

| Factor | EPF | PPF |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Who it usually fits | Salaried employees covered by the scheme | Individuals who open and fund a PPF account under scheme rules |
| Typical cash flow | Monthly deductions from salary + employer share to EPF/EPS as applicable | Yearly (or periodic) contributions you choose, within limits |
| Contribution driver | % of EPF wages (payroll) | Amount you decide to deposit each year |
| Planning tool | [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator) | [PPF Calculator](/calculators/ppf-calculator) |
| Horizon feel | Career-length service years | Classic long block (often modeled as 15 years, sometimes extended) |

Use the table as a lens for *your* cash flow — not as a ranking of which product is “better.”

## When EPF is the natural starting point

If you already see provident-fund deductions on your payslip, EPF is part of your compensation structure. Modeling it answers: *If my basic wages, contribution rates, and assumed interest stay in this ballpark for N years, what corpus might build?*

Practical steps:

1. Note monthly EPF wages (often basic + DA).
2. Confirm employee % and how your employer’s share is split between EPF and EPS (if applicable).
3. Run scenarios in the [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator) with and without a salary hike.
4. Compare the result to other goals funded by SIPs or FDs.

Deep dive on inputs and modes: [EPF Calculator Guide](/blog/epf-calculator-guide).

## When PPF fits alongside (or instead)

PPF planning fits people who can commit a **yearly** amount for a **long** horizon and want a structured savings habit that is not driven by an employer payroll percentage. You pick the yearly contribution and the rate assumption, then project maturity with the [PPF Calculator](/calculators/ppf-calculator).

Example habit:

- Choose a yearly contribution you can fund after expenses.
- Model the standard long block, then a longer tenure if you expect to extend.
- Keep rate assumptions for planning only — confirm the notified rate when you deposit.

More detail: [PPF Calculator Guide](/blog/ppf-calculator-guide).

## Can you use both?

Many households treat EPF as the **salary-linked** base and PPF (or SIPs) as **voluntary** top-ups. Whether both are available or optimal for *you* depends on eligibility, cash flow, and goals — not on a single blog rule. Numerically, you can:

- Project EPF with the [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator)
- Project PPF with the [PPF Calculator](/calculators/ppf-calculator)
- Add market-linked monthly investing with the [SIP Calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator)
- Benchmark fixed-rate bank deposits with the [FD Calculator](/calculators/fd-calculator)

Summing separate projections is a planning exercise; it is not a substitute for consolidated financial advice.

## How to decide what to model first

Ask three questions:

1. **Do I already contribute via payroll?** Start with EPF so you understand the money you already save.
2. **Do I have surplus I can lock for a long voluntary horizon?** Model PPF (and/or SIP) next.
3. **Is my goal date shorter or more flexible?** An FD or SIP tenure you choose may fit better than a long provident block — compare with FD and SIP calculators rather than forcing every rupee into one product label.
