**HRA** (House Rent Allowance) and **take-home pay** show up on the same payslip conversation — but they answer different questions. HRA exemption estimates how much of the HRA component may be **excluded from taxable salary** under Section 10(13A). Take-home pay is what lands in your bank after **taxes and deductions** across the whole CTC structure.

This article is educational planning content, not tax, legal, or payroll advice. Rules, metro classification, and employer salary definitions change — verify with official sources, HR, and a qualified advisor when decisions involve real money.

## Snapshot comparison

| Factor | HRA exemption | Take-home / net pay |
|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| What it usually is | Portion of HRA that may be tax-exempt | Cash after tax and common deductions |
| Main drivers | Basic + DA, HRA, rent, metro vs non-metro | Gross pay, tax regime/sketch, PF, other deductions |
| Typical formula feel | Minimum of three HRA limits | Gross − tax − deductions |

Use the table as a lens for *your* payslip — not as a ranking of which number matters more.

## When HRA is the natural starting point

If you are modeling a **rent and allowance** question — *If my rent, HRA, and city stay in this ballpark, how much HRA might be exempt?* — start with the [HRA Calculator](/calculators/hra-calculator).

Practical steps:

1. Pull basic, DA, and HRA from your latest payslip (same period).
2. Enter actual rent paid for that period.
3. Choose metro (50%) or non-metro (40%) for the city rule you are modeling.
4. Note which of the three limits is binding — that tells you what lever moves exemption most.

## How they fit together

Many people need **both** views:

- **HRA calculator** ≈ exempt vs taxable HRA for the allowance line
- **Salary / paycheck tools** ≈ how tax and deductions shape net cash

Raising rent can increase HRA exemption (via the rent − 10% salary limit) without changing gross CTC — but it also raises cash outflow for rent. That trade-off is why exemption and take-home should be read together, not as the same number.
