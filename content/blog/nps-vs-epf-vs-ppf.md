**NPS** (National Pension System), **EPF** (Employee Provident Fund), and **PPF** (Public Provident Fund) are three common long-horizon savings paths people compare when planning for later life. They are not interchangeable products. EPF is typically tied to **employment and payroll**. PPF is typically a **voluntary account** you fund on your schedule (within scheme rules). NPS is typically a **pension account** with market-linked fund options and contribution rules that can include employee and employer shares.

This article is educational planning content, not tax, legal, or investment advice. Scheme limits, interest or return notifications, tax treatment, exit rules, and eligibility change — verify with official sources and a qualified advisor when decisions involve real money.

## Snapshot comparison

| Factor | NPS | EPF | PPF |
|--------|-----|-----|-----|
| Who it usually fits | Individuals building a pension corpus (including many salaried / corporate subscribers) | Salaried employees covered by the scheme | Individuals who open and fund a PPF account under scheme rules |
| Typical cash flow | Monthly contributions (subscriber ± employer) | Monthly deductions from salary + employer share to EPF/EPS as applicable | Yearly (or periodic) contributions you choose, within limits |
| Return style | Market-linked via chosen funds / allocation | Notified interest credited per scheme rules | Notified interest (scheme rules) |
| Planning tool | [NPS Calculator](/calculators/nps-calculator) | [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator) | [PPF Calculator](/calculators/ppf-calculator) |
| Horizon feel | Career-length years to retirement age | Career-length service years | Classic long block (often modeled as 15 years, sometimes extended) |

Use the table as a lens for *your* cash flow and risk tolerance — not as a ranking of which product is “better.”

## When NPS is the natural starting point

If you already contribute to NPS (or your employer offers corporate NPS), modeling answers: *If my monthly contribution, assumed return, and years to retirement stay in this ballpark, what corpus might build — and how might lump-sum vs annuity purchase look?*

Practical steps:

1. Note total monthly contribution (your share + employer share if any).
2. Pick a blended expected return that matches the allocation you want to stress-test — not a promised rate.
3. Set current age and target retirement age; optionally add step-up and opening balance.
4. Run the [NPS Calculator](/calculators/nps-calculator) and save scenarios (conservative vs optimistic return).

## When EPF or PPF may dominate the conversation

- **EPF** fits when provident-fund deductions already appear on your payslip. Model wages, contribution %, and years of service with the [EPF Calculator](/calculators/epf-calculator).
- **PPF** fits when you control yearly voluntary deposits and want a notified-style rate model over a long block — use the [PPF Calculator](/calculators/ppf-calculator).

Many people use more than one path (for example EPF through payroll plus NPS or PPF as a voluntary layer). Calculators help you see rupee outcomes side by side instead of comparing slogans.

## How to compare scenarios fairly

1. Align time horizons (same number of years where possible).
2. Be honest about cash flow — monthly vs yearly deposits are not the same commitment.
3. Stress-test returns: try a lower and higher assumed rate for NPS; keep EPF/PPF rates as *assumptions you enter*, not defaults treated as official forever.
4. Separate **corpus size** from **liquidity and exit rules** — NPS annuity requirements, EPF withdrawal rules, and PPF lock-in feel different even at the same maturity number.
