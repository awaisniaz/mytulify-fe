**ROI** (return on investment) and **profit margin** both talk about “how good was the money outcome?” — but they divide by different bases. Confusing them leads to bad comparisons: a high margin product can still show weak ROI if it ties up a lot of capital, and a strong ROI campaign can look modest if you only stare at revenue margin.

This article is educational planning content, not investment, tax, or accounting advice. Definitions can vary by industry and reporting standard — verify with your books and a qualified advisor when decisions involve real money.

## Snapshot comparison

| Factor | ROI | Profit margin |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| Typical question | How efficient was my *investment / cost*? | How profitable was my *sale / price*? |
| Core formula | Net gain ÷ cost × 100 | Profit ÷ selling price × 100 |
| Denominator | What you spent or invested | What the customer paid (revenue) |
| Common use | Campaigns, projects, capital, portfolio moves | Pricing, product mix, sales health |
| Planning tool | [ROI Calculator](/calculators/roi-calculator) | [Profit Margin Calculator](/calculators/profit-margin-calculator) |
| Related idea | Return multiple, annualized ROI | Markup (profit ÷ cost) — different from margin |

Use the table as a lens for *your* decision — not as a claim that one metric is always “better.”

## When profit margin is the natural starting point

If you are asking *Of each dollar of selling price, how much is profit?* — use the [Profit Margin Calculator](/calculators/profit-margin-calculator). That tool separates **margin** (profit ÷ price) from **markup** (profit ÷ cost), which people often mix up the same way they mix ROI and margin.

Typical uses:

- Setting or checking list prices
- Comparing product lines on revenue profitability
- Finding the price needed for a target margin

## Same numbers, different stories

Suppose cost is 50 and selling price is 80:

- Profit = 30
- **Profit margin** = 30 ÷ 80 = **37.5%**
- **Markup** = 30 ÷ 50 = **60%**
- **ROI** (if the “investment” is that 50 cost) = 30 ÷ 50 = **60%**

Here ROI matches markup because both divide by cost — but ROI is still the wrong label if your dashboard is supposed to report *margin on sales*. Always name the denominator.

If you need a time-adjusted compound rate from beginning to ending value, use the [CAGR Calculator](/calculators/cagr-calculator) alongside ROI rather than treating CAGR and ROI as interchangeable without a holding period.

## Bottom line

Pick **ROI** when the story is *return on what I invested*. Pick **profit margin** when the story is *profit share of what I charged*.
