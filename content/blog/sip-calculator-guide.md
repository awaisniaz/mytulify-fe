A **SIP calculator** answers a simple planning question: if you invest a fixed amount every month at an assumed annual return, what could the pot be worth at the end? That projection helps you compare tenures, set goals, and decide whether a **mutual fund SIP** fits your cash flow — before you commit money.

## What a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is

A SIP is a habit of investing a fixed sum (often monthly) into a mutual fund scheme. Markets move; rupee-cost averaging means you buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high. Over long periods, compounding of returns can do more work than trying to time a single lump sum.

A calculator does **not** predict real markets. It assumes a constant annual rate so you can stress-test “what if” scenarios (for example 10%, 12%, or 14% expected return).

## The SIP future-value formula

For a fixed monthly investment **P**, monthly rate **r** (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and **n** months:

**FV = P × [((1 + r)^n − 1) / r] × (1 + r)**

- **Total invested** = P × n  
- **Estimated returns** = FV − total invested  
- **Maturity value** = FV  

When the rate is zero, maturity simply equals the sum of deposits.

### Quick example

₹10,000 per month for 10 years (120 months) at 12% annual return:

- Monthly rate ≈ 1%  
- Total invested = ₹12,00,000  
- Projected maturity is far higher than the sum of deposits because each installment compounds for a different length of time

## Why expected return and tenure matter more than vibes

| Input | What it changes |
|-------|-----------------|
| Monthly SIP | Scales invested amount and maturity almost linearly |
| Expected return % | Compounding sensitivity — small rate gaps grow large over decades |
| Tenure (years) | Time in market; longer SIPs usually dominate higher short-term rates |
| Annual step-up % | Raises later-year deposits as income grows |

Aggressive return assumptions look great on a chart and disappoint in real life. Prefer a conservative expected return for goals you cannot miss (education, home down payment). For stretch goals, run a second scenario at a higher rate and plan for the gap.

## Step-up SIP: grow the contribution with your income

Many investors start with an affordable monthly SIP, then increase it by a fixed percent each year (often 5–10%) when salary rises. That **annual step-up** keeps the habit aligned with earning power and usually builds a larger corpus than a flat SIP with the same starting amount.

## Practical tips before you start a real SIP

- Match SIP date to payday so contributions feel automatic.  
- Prefer goal-linked tenures (5 / 10 / 15 / 20 years) over “until I feel rich.”  
- Revisit the calculator yearly when income or goals change.  
- After the fact, compare fund performance with [CAGR](/calculators/cagr-calculator) on beginning vs ending values — SIP projections and historical CAGR answer different questions.
