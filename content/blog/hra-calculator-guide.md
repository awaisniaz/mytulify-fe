An **HRA calculator** helps salaried employees estimate how much of their **House Rent Allowance** may be tax-exempt under Section 10(13A), using the familiar three-limit method. Instead of guessing from a Form 16 line, you model **basic**, **dearness allowance (DA)**, **HRA received**, **rent paid**, and whether you live in a **metro** or **non-metro** city.

This is a **planning** view. Salary definitions, metro classification, and tax treatment depend on current law and how your employer structures the CTC. Always confirm with payroll / a qualified tax advisor — calculator output is a scenario, not a filing instruction.

## What an HRA calculator needs

| Input | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| Basic salary | Part of “salary” for the HRA rules |
| Dearness allowance | Usually included with basic for HRA salary |
| HRA received | The allowance actually paid in the period |
| Rent paid | Actual rent for the same monthly or yearly period |
| Metro vs non-metro | Chooses the 50% or 40% of salary ceiling |

Open the [HRA Calculator](/calculators/hra-calculator) and keep every money field on the **same period** (all monthly, or all yearly).

## How the exemption estimate is built (plain language)

Exempt HRA is the **minimum** of three limits:

1. **Actual HRA received** for the period
2. **Rent paid − 10% of salary**, where salary is typically basic + DA
3. **50% of salary** if metro (classic list: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) or **40% of salary** if non-metro

**Taxable HRA** = HRA received − exempt HRA.

That breakdown is enough to compare scenarios — for example “raise rent” vs “move cities” vs “HRA component change” — without uploading payslips.

### Worked planning example

Suppose (monthly):

- Basic: ₹50,000
- DA: ₹5,000 → salary for HRA = ₹55,000
- HRA received: ₹20,000
- Rent paid: ₹25,000
- City: metro (50%)

Then:

- Limit 1 (actual HRA) = ₹20,000
- Limit 2 (rent − 10% salary) = 25,000 − 5,500 = ₹19,500
- Limit 3 (50% of salary) = ₹27,500

The **minimum** is ₹19,500 → that is the exempt illustration; taxable HRA illustration = 20,000 − 19,500 = ₹500. Run the same numbers in the [HRA Calculator](/calculators/hra-calculator) and change one lever at a time.

## When rent is low relative to salary

If rent is only slightly above (or below) 10% of basic + DA, **limit 2** can shrink or hit zero. In that case exemption cannot exceed the rent-based limit even when HRA and the city % look generous. That is why the tool shows all three limits and which one is **binding**.
