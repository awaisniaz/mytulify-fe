**Debt-to-income ratio** is monthly debt divided by gross monthly income. There are two versions, and mixing them up makes a budget look safer than a lender’s worksheet.

**Front-end DTI** is the housing payment alone ÷ gross income. Housing here means the full payment: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, HOA, and PMI. Get that figure from the [Mortgage Calculator](/calculators/mortgage-calculator), not from principal and interest only.

**Back-end DTI** adds car loans, student loans, personal loans, and credit card minimums. A planning rule of thumb is about 28% front-end and 36% back-end, with many programs still considering files up to about 43% back-end. Those are guidelines. Credit, reserves, and the loan type move the line.

The [Debt-to-Income Calculator](/calculators/debt-to-income-calculator) shows both percentages from three inputs. Use gross pay, before tax. If the back-end ratio is high because of cards, the [Credit Card Payoff Calculator](/calculators/credit-card-payoff-calculator) shows what a larger payment does to the balance.
