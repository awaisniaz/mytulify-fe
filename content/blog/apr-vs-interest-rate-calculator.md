The interest rate sets the payment on the amount you borrow. **APR** answers a different question: what rate makes those same payments equal the cash you actually receive after upfront fees?

You borrow $20,000, the lender keeps $600 in fees, and you leave with $19,400. The payment is still calculated on $20,000. APR is the monthly rate, annualized, at which the present value of the payments equals $19,400. When fees are zero, APR matches the note rate. When fees are large, APR is higher. That gap is the point of comparing offers.

The [APR Calculator](/calculators/apr-calculator) shows the payment, the amount received, and the APR. Use the [Loan Calculator](/calculators/loan-calculator) or [Mortgage Calculator](/calculators/mortgage-calculator) when you care about the schedule and housing costs. A credit card APR is already the revolving rate — payoff time for that balance is the [Credit Card Payoff Calculator](/calculators/credit-card-payoff-calculator), not this fee-based APR.

APR here does not include optional products you can decline, and it is not the APR a lender must disclose. It is the math behind that disclosure so you can see why two “7.5%” loans are not equal.
